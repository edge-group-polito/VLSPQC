## (a) Paragraph

We benchmarked the level-1 variants of FAEST (v3), MAYO, MQOM, QR-UOV, SDitH, SNOVA, SQIsign and UOV (39 variants) using the reference C code of each package, compiled with plain gcc \texttt{-O2}, with one operation per process, pinned to one core, and a 1000-byte message. Table~\ref{tab:l1} reports the per-metric median of three runs. FAEST has the smallest public key of all variants (32 B) and the smallest key material (pk+sk of 64 B; next: MQOM-gf2-shorter, 124 B); only MAYO has a smaller secret key (24 B). Its signatures are large: 3466 to 5170 B, against 2492 to 3316 B for MQOM, 3721 to 4914 B for SDitH, and at most 541 B for MAYO, QR-UOV, SNOVA, SQIsign and UOV. Key generation takes 0.023 to 0.032 ms, in the same range as SDitH (0.032 to 0.038 ms) and at least 15 times less than MAYO, MQOM, SNOVA, SQIsign, UOV and QR-UOV (0.49 ms and above). Signing and verification, however, are slow in the reference code: the fastest variant, FAEST-EM-128f, needs 505 ms to sign and 580 ms to verify, i.e. about 16 and 21 times more than the best SDitH variant, 8.5 and 78 times more than SQIsign, and 80 to 2000 times more (sign) than the best MQOM, SNOVA, QR-UOV, MAYO and UOV variants; the s variants need 4.2 to 6.3 s to sign. Signing with FAEST needs between 271 and 2712 KiB of heap plus stack (the f variants 271 to 345 KiB), comparable to MQOM and SDitH.

## (c) Discussion (Section III-A style)

FAEST stands out for its keys: a 32-byte public key and a 32-byte secret key are the smallest pk+sk of all level-1 variants, and its key generation (tens of microseconds) is among the fastest, together with SDitH. The price is paid in the signature (3466 to 5170 B; only SDitH and MQOM are of the same order) and above all in signing and verification time: in this reference implementation FAEST-EM-128f is the fastest FAEST variant at 505 ms (sign) and 580 ms (verify), and the s variants trade 17\% to 21\% smaller signatures for about 8 times more time (FAEST-EM-128s: 3466 B, 4.15 s sign; FAEST-128s: 4066 B, 6.31 s). The EM variants are faster and smaller than the corresponding 128f/128s variants (sign 505 ms vs. 763 ms, signature 4170 B vs. 5170 B). Compared with the other signature-size classes, SDitH (similar signature size) signs about 16 times faster than the best FAEST variant, and SQIsign (200 B) about 8.5 times faster at signing but with a much slower key generation (20 ms); the multivariate schemes (MAYO, SNOVA, QR-UOV, UOV) and MQOM sign two to three orders of magnitude faster. Run-to-run variation (up to 56\% for FAEST timings) is much smaller than these factors, so they are not affected by it; the 1.4 times key-generation difference to SDitH is, and is not claimed. UOV-Is-pkc shows the opposite trade-off to FAEST: a 96 B signature, but a 66576 B public key. The FAEST code is the unoptimised v3 reference implementation; optimised (AES-NI/AVX2) FAEST implementations are far faster and are not measured here.

## (d) Methodology and caveats (to be placed in the setup paragraph / footnote)

All schemes are measured with their reference, non-optimised, portable C implementations (no AVX2, AES-NI or other platform-specific paths, no external crypto library), gcc 13.3 \texttt{-O2}, on an Intel Core Ultra 7 155H under WSL2 (hybrid P/E-core CPU), with each operation in its own process pinned to one logical CPU. The time of an operation is the median over up to 1000 calls (at most 3 s budget, at least 5 calls) of a 1000-byte message. Absolute numbers are therefore not comparable with those of papers that benchmark optimised packages, and cycle counts are raw TSC ticks, relative only under WSL2. We repeated the whole benchmark three times (sequentially, machine otherwise idle apart from a VS Code C/C++ language-server process of about one core, mean user CPU 10--11\% per vmstat, see `meta.json` of each run); the per-metric (max-min)/median spread of the timings over the three runs has a median of 31\% over variants and operations (maximum 80\%; FAEST: median 34\%, maximum 56\%). Differences between schemes below about 1.5 times are therefore not claimed. Heap, stack, code size and all key/signature sizes were identical in all three runs.

Correctness checks and provenance of the ported sources (to be summarised in a footnote or the artefact description):
- FAEST v3 (4 level-1 variants): the port equals the official v3 KAT byte-for-byte on 100 records per variant.
- QR-UOV: equals the official Round-3 KAT (QR_UOV-Round3-KAT) byte-for-byte on 10 records for all 30 variants and on 100 records for the 10 level-1 variants.
- MAYO, SNOVA and SDitH match the official KAT on 10 records; SQIsign on 2 to 3 records; MQOM (all 18 variants) on record 0 only.
- UOV (6 level-1 variants) has no official KAT available offline; it was checked against the package's own generator on records 0 and 1 only.
- The MQOM package directories were empty; the sources come from a complete earlier copy of unknown archive origin, whose record 0 matches the official KAT.
- SQIsign (static build) needs \texttt{-I.} and SDitH needs \texttt{-lm}.
- Memory figures are heap (allocator wrapper) and stack (page granularity, 4 KiB) of the scheme; the harness's own key and message buffers are not charged, so UOV and QR-UOV figures exclude key storage. Stack values of 4 KiB and below are floors, not measurements. VmHWM (in `results.csv`) is indicative only and not used in the text.
- Each metric of each row is the median over the three runs separately, so a row's values may come from different runs.
